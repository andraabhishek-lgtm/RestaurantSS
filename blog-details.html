The Art of A5 Wagyu: Why Japan's Finest Beef Changed My Life as a Chef

Chef Laurent Dubois

Executive Chef

· June 15, 2024 · 8 min read · 4,248 views

When I first tasted A5 Wagyu in a small, unremarkable restaurant in Kagoshima, Japan in 2009, I cried. I'm not ashamed to admit it. I was 28 years old, had trained at three Michelin-starred establishments in Lyon and Paris, had spent six months in Tokyo learning kaiseki fundamentals — and nothing had ever made me cry at a table before.

But this was different. This wasn't just beef. It wasn't even really food in the way I understood food. It was a philosophical statement, a quiet declaration that patience, precision, and reverence for nature could produce something so profoundly perfect that it transcended the act of eating entirely.

"Great cooking is not about technique alone. It is about understanding that every ingredient is a story, and your job as a chef is to tell that story as clearly and as beautifully as you can."

Understanding A5: More Than Just Marbling

The "A5" designation is the highest grade given to Japanese Wagyu by the Japanese Meat Grading Association. It represents a perfect score in both yield grade (A) and meat quality grade (5). But explaining it in those terms completely misses the point — like describing the Mona Lisa by its canvas dimensions.

True A5 Wagyu, specifically from Kagoshima, Kobe, or Miyazaki Prefecture, has a Beef Marbling Score (BMS) of 8 or higher. The intramuscular fat — called "sashi" — doesn't just marble the meat; it creates a completely different structural composition. The fat melts at body temperature. This means the moment it touches your tongue, it begins to dissolve.

The Preparation: Restraint as Artistry

One of the hardest lessons I've learned as a chef is knowing when to step back. With A5 Wagyu, the greatest gift you can give the ingredient is to get out of its way. My preparation philosophy for our signature tenderloin is built on restraint:

  • Season only with Maldon sea salt and freshly ground white pepper — nothing else
  • Bring to room temperature for exactly 45 minutes before cooking
  • Sear on a cast-iron skillet preheated to 700°F for 90 seconds per side
  • Rest for 10 full minutes, tented loosely — this is non-negotiable
  • Serve at exactly 52°C internal temperature (125°F, rare-plus)

The accompaniments exist to provide contrast and context, not to compete. Our truffle reduction is subtle — a whisper, not a shout. The roasted bone marrow adds a secondary layer of richness. The micro herbs provide freshness that cuts through the fat. It's a composition, not a collection.

Why This Dish Defines ÉCLAT

When we opened ÉCLAT in 2018, I knew the A5 Wagyu Tenderloin would be our centerpiece. Not because wagyu is fashionable — though it certainly is — but because it represents everything I believe fine dining should be: an experience that makes you stop, be present, and feel something.

In the years since we opened, I have watched hundreds of guests take their first bite. The reaction is almost always the same — a pause, a slight widening of the eyes, sometimes a sound of involuntary pleasure. Sometimes, yes, a tear. I understand now. That moment in Kagoshima wasn't about weakness. It was about being fully, viscerally present in the face of something extraordinary.

That is what I hope ÉCLAT gives you, every single time.
